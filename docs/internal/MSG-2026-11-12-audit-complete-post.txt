🔷 RiskShard Update (Open cyber-loss evidence)

The source audit is finished, of everything we could obtain.

76 public cyber-loss reports registered. 66 read against the publisher's own
file. The other 10 we could not obtain, and each one records why.

Four questions, asked of every one of the 66.

Does it publish a most-likely value.
Does it publish a distribution, or only point statistics.
Does it say how often a loss of a given size is exceeded.
Can you name the population that was measured, from the report itself.

15 of the 66 publish a distribution. 21 state an exceedance over a named
population. 13 do not let you name the population at all.

That last one is the filter most of us skip. If you cannot say who was
measured, you cannot say whether the figure describes companies like yours.

For nine of the ten we could not read, what is public is a page about the
report rather than the report — a summary, a press release, a landing page.
The tenth was retired by its publisher. Each is recorded as a gap with a
reason rather than left blank.

One publisher is excluded outright, on a conflict of mine. The name is
withheld, the exclusion is published, and the gap is counted.

Every answer is pinned to the sha256 of the file it was read from, so next
year's edition goes stale loudly instead of quietly. The reading is extracted
text, which means a figure printed only inside a chart image would not have
been seen, and every record says so.

Next is the denominator. For every headline figure, what it was divided by,
and whether the publisher stated it or you have to work it out.

Link in the first comment.
